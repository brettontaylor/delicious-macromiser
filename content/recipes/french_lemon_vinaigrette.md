# Classic French Lemon Vinaigrette

> Lemon-forward, fully classical. Bright acid, clean emulsion, no shortcuts.

**Yield:** Dresses a generous salad for 2  
**Active time:** 5 minutes

---

## Ingredients

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fresh lemon juice | 1 tbsp |
| White wine vinegar (artisanal) | ½ tbsp |
| Dijon mustard | 1 tsp |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 3½ tbsp |
| Flaky salt | 2 pinches |
| Black pepper | freshly cracked, to finish |

*Optional: ¼ tsp dry mustard powder alongside the Dijon for a sharper emulsion*

---

## Method

1. **Salt the acid first.** Combine lemon juice, white wine vinegar, and salt in a bowl. Rest 2 minutes — salt must dissolve fully in the acid, not the oil.

2. **Add mustard.** Whisk into the acid until fully combined. The mustard coats the bowl and gives the oil something to bind to.

3. **Emulsify.** Add olive oil in a thin, steady stream while whisking constantly. Take about 60 seconds. The finished dressing should be slightly thickened, opaque, and coat a spoon cleanly — not separated, not creamy-thick.

4. **Taste on a leaf.** Dip a leaf of whatever you're dressing and taste it — not the dressing alone. Adjust: a drop more lemon if flat, a pinch more salt if hollow, a drop more vinegar if it needs edge.

5. **Dress at the last moment.** Toss greens immediately before serving. Finish with a crack of black pepper directly on the leaves.

---

## Notes

- **Acid:oil ratio** is 1:2.3 — slightly leaner than standard to keep the lemon prominent
- **Mustard:** Dijon only. Whole grain muddies the texture for a refined dressing
- **For robust greens** (arugula, radicchio, endive): reduce lemon by ¼ and lean harder on the vinegar
- **Warm application:** excellent over steamed haricots verts or asparagus
- **Best greens:** frisée, butter lettuce, Little Gem, endive
