Spicy Dry-Rubbed Baked Chicken

Smoked Paprika · Cayenne · Garlic · Crispy Skin · High Heat
10 min
Prep
·
40–50 min
Bake
·
425°F
Oven
·
4–6
Serves
Ingredients
  • 4bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
  • 2–4bone-in, skin-on chicken breasts
  • 2 tbspolive oil
— Dry Rub —
  • 2 tspsmoked paprika
  • 1 tspcayenne pepper
  • 1 tspgarlic powder
  • 1 tsponion powder
  • ½ tspground cumin
  • ½ tspdried oregano
  • 1 tspbrown sugar
  • 1 tbspkosher salt
  • 1 tspblack pepper, coarse
Method
  1. 1
    Preheat & Mix Rub
    Heat oven to 425°F with a rack in the upper-middle position. In a small bowl, combine 2 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp cayenne, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp onion powder, ½ tsp cumin, ½ tsp dried oregano, 1 tsp brown sugar, 1 tbsp kosher salt, and 1 tsp black pepper. Stir until evenly mixed.
  2. 2
    Pat Dry & Oil
    Pat all chicken pieces completely dry with paper towels — wet skin is the enemy of crunch. Drizzle 2 tbsp olive oil over the chicken and rub it all over every surface, including under the skin where you can get your fingers in.
  3. 3
    Coat with Rub
    Press the spice rub generously onto every surface of the chicken — top, bottom, sides, and under the skin. Don't be shy. If time allows, let the coated pieces sit uncovered at room temp for 20–30 minutes, or refrigerate uncovered up to 4 hours — the dry air further crisps the skin.
  4. 4
    Bake — Skin Side Up
    Arrange chicken skin-side up on a wire rack set over a rimmed sheet pan. Never crowd the pieces. Breasts are done at 165°F internal, around 35–40 minutes. Thighs read better at 170–175°F for texture — about 45–50 minutes. Check with an instant-read thermometer, not the clock.
  5. 5
    Rest & Serve
    Pull the chicken and let it rest 5 minutes — don't skip this. The skin should be deep red-brown and crackling. Serve immediately; the crust softens as it sits.
Chef's Note
The wire rack is not optional — direct pan contact steams the underside into mush. If you only have a sheet pan, prop the pieces on thick-sliced onion rounds; they'll char under the chicken and the skin will still crisp. Don't cover or tent the chicken at any point.

Sides & Plating

Roasted Potatoes & Cucumber Salad

Crisp · Garlicky · Cool Contrast
Garlic Roasted Potatoes
  • 1½ lbsbaby potatoes, halved
  • 3 clovesgarlic, smashed
  • 2 tbspolive oil
  • ½ tspsmoked paprika
  • ¾ tspkosher salt
  • ½ tspblack pepper
  • 4 sprigsfresh thyme or rosemary
  1. 1
    Season & Roast
    Toss 1½ lbs halved baby potatoes with 2 tbsp olive oil, 3 smashed garlic cloves, ½ tsp smoked paprika, ¾ tsp kosher salt, and ½ tsp pepper. Spread cut-side down on a sheet pan with the herb sprigs. Roast at 425°F for 30–35 minutes alongside the chicken until golden and crisp-edged. No stirring — let the cut side caramelize undisturbed.
Cucumber & Red Onion Salad
  • 1English cucumber, thin-sliced
  • ¼red onion, paper-thin sliced
  • 2 tbspred wine vinegar
  • 1 tbspolive oil
  • ½ tspkosher salt
  • ½ tspsugar
  • 2 tbspfresh dill or flat-leaf parsley
  1. 1
    Quick Pickle & Toss
    Combine 2 tbsp red wine vinegar, 1 tbsp olive oil, ½ tsp salt, and ½ tsp sugar in a bowl. Add the thin-sliced cucumber and red onion and toss. Let sit at least 10 minutes — 30 is better. Fold in fresh dill or parsley just before serving. This salad is the cooling foil to the spicy chicken; don't skip it.
To Plate
Place one or two pieces of chicken skin-side up — never face-down, never under sauce. Fan the roasted potatoes alongside, cut-side visible. Add a generous scoop of cucumber salad on the opposite side of the plate; the cool acidity cuts the heat beautifully. Finish the chicken with a squeeze of fresh lemon right before service. Serve on warm plates so the skin doesn't go cold and limp before the first bite.
Make Ahead
The dry rub keeps in a sealed jar for months — make a double or triple batch. Chicken can be rubbed up to 24 hours in advance and held uncovered on a rack in the fridge; the exposed skin dries out even further and produces a superior crust. The cucumber salad improves after 30 minutes of sitting and can be made up to 2 hours ahead (drain lightly before serving if it releases too much liquid).
Wine Pairing
Spice and tannin fight — avoid big Cabernet. A medium-bodied Grenache or Côtes du Rhône is the move: enough fruit to meet the heat, enough weight to hold its own. For white, an off-dry Riesling or a round, textured Roussanne handles the cayenne without flattening it. Rosé from Provence — dry and minerally — is also an excellent call here.
