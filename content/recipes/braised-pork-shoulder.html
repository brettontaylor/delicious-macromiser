Red Wine Braised
Pork Shoulder

Soffritto · San Marzano · Fresh Herbs · Finished with Butter
45 min
Prep
·
4 hrs
Braise
·
325°F
Oven
·
8
Serves
Ingredients
  • 5.5 lbPork shoulder (Boston butt)
  • 2 tbspKosher salt
  • 1½ tspBlack pepper, cracked
  • 3 tbspOlive oil
  • 2Yellow onions, roughly chopped
  • 3Carrots, roughly chopped
  • 3Celery stalks, roughly chopped
  • 8 clovesGarlic, smashed
  • 3 tbspTomato paste
  • 1 tspSmoked paprika
  • ½ tspRed pepper flakes
  • 2 cupsDry red wine (Malbec / Rhône)
  • 2 cupsChicken or pork stock, low-sodium
  • 14 ozCanned San Marzano tomatoes, crushed
  • 3 sprigsFresh rosemary
  • 5 sprigsFresh thyme
  • 3Bay leaves
— To Finish —
  • 1 tbspBalsamic vinegar
  • 2 tbspCold unsalted butter
Method
  1. 1
    Season & Rest
    Pat pork completely dry. Season all sides with 2 tbsp salt and 1½ tsp pepper. Rest at room temperature 45 min (or overnight uncovered in fridge).
  2. 2
    Preheat & Sear
    Preheat oven to 325°F, rack in lower third. Heat 3 tbsp olive oil in a 7–8 qt Dutch oven over medium-high until smoking. Sear pork on all sides until deep mahogany, 4–5 min per side. Remove and set aside.
  3. 3
    Soffritto
    Reduce to medium. Add onions, carrots, and celery. Cook 8–10 min until softened. Add garlic, cook 2 more minutes.
  4. 4
    Build the Base
    Push veg to edges. Add 3 tbsp tomato paste to the center; cook stirring 2–3 min until it darkens. Add smoked paprika and pepper flakes. Stir everything together.
  5. 5
    Deglaze
    Pour in 2 cups wine. Scrape up all browned bits. Simmer and reduce by one-third, about 6 min.
  6. 6
    Braise
    Add 2 cups stock and 14 oz crushed tomatoes. Nestle in rosemary, thyme, and bay leaves. Return pork fat-cap UP. Liquid should reach halfway up the pork. Cover and braise 3.5–4 hrs, flipping once at the halfway point.
  7. 7
    Rest & Finish Sauce
    Remove pork; tent with foil 20–30 min. Discard herb sprigs and bay leaves. Skim fat, then reduce sauce on stovetop 10–15 min. Off heat, stir in 1 tbsp balsamic and 2 tbsp cold butter. Shred pork and return to pot.
Chef's Note
Use a wine you'd drink — never cooking wine. Malbec, Côtes du Rhône, or Nero d'Avola are ideal. This braise is markedly better the next day: refrigerate overnight, lift off the solidified fat cap, and reheat gently.

Classic Accompaniment

Creamy Parmesan Polenta

The natural pairing — rich, yielding, built to catch the sauce
Ingredients — Serves 8
  • 4 cupsWhole milk
  • 4 cupsWater or light chicken stock
  • 2 tspKosher salt
  • 1½ cupsCoarse-ground polenta (not instant)
  • 4 tbspUnsalted butter, cold-cubed
  • 2 ozMascarpone (optional, for extra richness)
  • 3 ozParmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
  • ½ tspWhite pepper
— Gremolata Garnish —
  • 1 bunchFlat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
  • 2 clovesGarlic, microplaned
  • 1Lemon, zested
  • PinchFlaky sea salt (Maldon)
Method
  1. 1
    Heat the Liquid
    Combine 4 cups whole milk, 4 cups water (or stock), and 2 tsp salt in a heavy-bottomed saucepan. Bring to a gentle boil over medium heat, stirring occasionally to prevent scorching.
  2. 2
    Whisk in the Polenta
    Reduce heat to medium-low. Slowly pour in 1½ cups polenta in a thin, steady stream while whisking constantly to prevent lumps. Switch to a wooden spoon once incorporated.
  3. 3
    Cook Low & Slow
    Cook on the lowest heat, stirring every 2–3 minutes, for 35–45 minutes. The polenta is ready when it pulls cleanly away from the sides of the pan and a spoon dragged through holds its line. Add a splash of milk or stock if it tightens too much.
  4. 4
    Finish & Season
    Off heat, stir in 4 tbsp cold butter, 2 oz mascarpone (if using), and 3 oz grated Parmigiano. Season with ½ tsp white pepper. Taste and adjust salt. The texture should be pourable but not thin — it will set slightly on the plate.
  5. 5
    Make Gremolata
    Combine finely chopped parsley, microplaned garlic, and lemon zest. Mix well. Scatter over the finished plate just before serving — this bright, raw garnish cuts through the richness of both the pork and polenta beautifully.
To Plate
Spoon a generous mound of polenta into a wide, shallow bowl. Nestle two or three large pieces of braised pork on top. Ladle plenty of sauce over everything — don't be shy, the sauce is the star. Finish with a pinch of gremolata and a crack of black pepper. Serve immediately; polenta waits for no one.
Make Ahead
Braise the pork a full day ahead — flavor improves overnight. Polenta is best made fresh, but can be reheated with a splash of milk over low heat, stirring vigorously.
Wine at the Table
Mirror what's in the braise: Malbec, a Rhône Grenache blend, or a southern Italian red (Nero d'Avola, Primitivo). Avoid heavy tannic Cabernets — the dish needs fruit-forward, not grip.
