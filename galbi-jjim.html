Ribs & Marinade
  • 1 lbBone-in beef short ribs
  • ¼ cupSoy sauce, low-sodium
  • 2 tbspMirin
  • 1 tbspRice wine / sake
  • ½ tbspBrown sugar or honey
  • ½ tbspSesame oil
  • ½Asian pear, grated
  • ½ smallKiwi, grated
  • 3 clovesGarlic, grated
  • ½ tbspFresh ginger, grated
  • ½ tspBlack pepper
— Braise Day —
  • 1 cupBeef stock
  • 3 ozDaikon, cut in 1½" rounds
  • 1 mediumCarrot, cut in 1½" pieces
  • 4–6Chestnuts, vacuum-packed
  • 2Shiitake mushrooms, quartered
  • 1 knotScallion (remove before serving)
— Garnish —
  • 1Egg (for jidan threads)
  • PinchToasted sesame seeds
  • PinchPine nuts, toasted
  • 2Scallions, julienned & curled in ice water
Night Before
  1. 1
    Soak
    Place 1 lb short ribs in a bowl, cover with cold water. Soak 1–2 hrs, changing water once to draw out blood for a cleaner braise. Drain.
  2. 2
    Blanch
    Transfer ribs to boiling water, blanch 5 minutes. Drain, rinse under cold water, pat completely dry.
  3. 3
    Marinade
    Whisk together ¼ cup soy sauce, 2 tbsp mirin, 1 tbsp rice wine, ½ tbsp brown sugar, ½ tbsp sesame oil, ½ grated pear, ½ grated kiwi, 3 cloves grated garlic, ½ tbsp ginger, and ½ tsp black pepper.
  4. 4
    Marinate
    Add ribs, turn to coat. Transfer to a zip-lock bag, press out air, and refrigerate overnight — minimum 8 hrs, up to 24.
Braise Day
  1. 5
    Temper & Sear
    Remove ribs from fridge 30 min before cooking. Preheat oven to 300°F. Heat neutral oil in a Dutch oven over high heat until smoking. Sear ribs on all sides until deep mahogany — 3–4 min per side. Do not rush.
  2. 6
    Braise — First Stage
    Add reserved marinade, 1 cup beef stock, and the scallion knot. Liquid should reach ¾ up the ribs. Bring to a simmer, cover, and transfer to oven. Braise covered for 2 hours.
  3. 7
    Add Veg — Second Stage
    Uncover and add 3 oz daikon, 1 carrot, 4–6 chestnuts, and 2 quartered shiitakes. Continue braising uncovered 45–60 min until ribs are tender and sauce is glossy. Skim fat. Remove scallion knot.
  4. 8
    Finish
    Taste and adjust — a pinch of sugar or a few drops of sesame oil will round the sauce if needed. The sauce should be deeply savory, glossy, and cling to the ribs.
Chef's Note
The Asian pear is non-negotiable — its enzymes tenderize the meat and balance the soy. Bosc pear works as a backup but is less effective. This dish is better the next day: reheat gently with a splash of stock, the sauce deepens beautifully overnight.

Sides & Finishing

Rice · Bok Choy · Plating & Garnish

Complete the bowl — every element earns its place
Steamed Short-Grain Rice
  • 1 cupShort-grain white rice
  • 1¼ cupsCold water
  1. 1
    Rinse
    Rinse 1 cup rice under cold water, swirling with your hand, until water runs nearly clear — about 3–4 changes. Drain well.
  2. 2
    Cook
    Combine rice and 1¼ cups cold water in a saucepan. Bring to a boil uncovered. Cover tightly, reduce to the lowest possible heat, and cook 12 minutes. Do not lift the lid.
  3. 3
    Steam & Fluff
    Remove from heat, still covered, and rest 10 minutes. Uncover and fluff gently with a rice paddle or fork before serving.
Garlic Sesame Bok Choy
  • 2Baby bok choy, halved lengthwise
  • 2 clovesGarlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 tspSoy sauce
  • ½ tspSesame oil
  • —Neutral oil, for the pan
  1. 1
    Blanch
    Halve 2 baby bok choy lengthwise. Blanch in salted boiling water 60–90 seconds until just wilted and bright green. Drain and pat completely dry.
  2. 2
    Sauté & Finish
    Heat a thin layer of neutral oil in a pan over medium-high. Sauté 2 cloves sliced garlic for 30 seconds until fragrant. Add bok choy cut-side down, sear 1 minute until lightly caramelized. Add 1 tsp soy sauce and ½ tsp sesame oil, toss to coat, and serve immediately.
Egg Jidan — Garnish Threads
Separate 1 egg. Season yolk and white each with a pinch of salt. Cook each separately as a thin crepe in a lightly oiled pan over very low heat — no color. Roll each crepe tightly and slice into fine julienne threads. Drape over the finished bowl.
Scallion Garnish
Julienne 2 scallions into fine 2-inch lengths. Submerge in a bowl of ice water for 10–15 minutes — they will curl into elegant ringlets. Drain and pat dry before plating. Combined with toasted sesame seeds and pine nuts, these garnishes transform a home braise into a composed dish.
To Plate
Place 1–2 ribs in a wide, shallow bowl with daikon rounds, carrot pieces, and chestnuts alongside. Ladle the braising sauce generously over everything — the glossy sauce is the centerpiece. Add a scoop of rice on the side or beneath the ribs. Place the bok choy at the edge of the bowl. Finish with jidan threads draped over the top, a scatter of toasted pine nuts and sesame seeds, and a nest of curled scallion julienne. Serve immediately.
Make Ahead
Galbi jjim is significantly better the next day. Refrigerate overnight, skim the solidified fat cleanly from the top, and reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of beef stock. The sauce deepens and the meat relaxes further into its braising liquid.
Key Notes
Bone-in timing: Adds gelatin — don't rush the uncovered final stage. Asian pear: Essential; enzymes tenderize and balance the soy. Do not skip the fruit. Chestnuts: Vacuum-packed are traditional — find them at any Asian grocery.
