Sirloin Tagliata

Screaming Hot Sear · Butter Baste · Sliced Against the Grain
15 min
Prep
·
5 min
Sear
·
5 min
Rest
·
2
Serves
Ingredients
  • 1–1½ lbVery thin-cut sirloin tip steak
  • 1½ tspKosher salt
  • ½ tspBlack pepper, cracked
  • 1 tbspAvocado or grapeseed oil (high smoke)
  • 2 tbspUnsalted butter
  • 2 sprigsFresh thyme
  • 1 cloveGarlic, smashed
— Lemon-Dijon Vinaigrette —
  • 2 tbspFresh lemon juice
  • 1 tspDijon mustard
  • ½ tspHoney
  • 4 tbspExtra-virgin olive oil
  • to tasteSalt and pepper
Method
  1. 1
    Season & Rest
    Pat steak completely dry — moisture is the enemy of crust. Season all sides with 1½ tsp kosher salt and ½ tsp cracked pepper. Rest at room temperature for 15 min.
  2. 2
    Make the Vinaigrette
    Whisk together 2 tbsp lemon juice, 1 tsp Dijon, and ½ tsp honey. Slowly drizzle in 4 tbsp olive oil while whisking to emulsify. Season with salt and pepper. Set aside.
  3. 3
    Get the Pan Screaming
    Heat a cast iron or heavy stainless skillet over high heat for 4–5 full minutes — it should just begin to smoke. Add 1 tbsp oil and swirl to coat. This step cannot be rushed.
  4. 4
    Sear — One Flip Only
    Lay steak flat; do not touch or move it. For very thin cuts, sear 60–90 seconds per side. If you have multiple pieces, work in batches — never crowd the pan or the temperature drops and you'll steam instead of sear.
  5. 5
    Butter Baste
    After flipping, add 2 tbsp butter, 2 sprigs thyme, and 1 smashed garlic clove. Tilt the pan toward you and continuously spoon the foaming butter over the steak for the remaining 60–90 seconds.
  6. 6
    Rest & Slice
    Transfer to a cutting board and tent loosely with foil. Rest 5 minutes — non-negotiable. Slice thinly against the grain at a slight diagonal. The slices should fan beautifully; this is the tagliata.
Chef's Note
The single most important step is the pan preheat — 4 to 5 full minutes, until it visibly smokes before the oil goes in. A warm pan gives you grey, steamed meat. A smoking pan gives you a mahogany crust in 90 seconds. With thin-cut steak there is no recovery from undercooking the pan.

Smashed Potatoes & Arugula Salad

The natural companions — one crispy and earthy, one bright and bitter
Smashed Crispy Potatoes — Serves 2
  • 3Medium Yukon Gold potatoes
  • 3 tbspOlive oil
  • 1 tspKosher salt
  • ½ tspBlack pepper
  • 3 clovesGarlic, smashed
  • 2 sprigsFresh rosemary or thyme
  1. 1
    Boil
    Preheat oven to 450°F. Boil potatoes whole in heavily salted water until completely fork-tender, 20–25 min. Drain and let steam dry on the pan for 5 min.
  2. 2
    Smash & Oil
    Transfer to an oiled baking sheet. Use a flat-bottomed glass to smash each potato to about ½ inch thick. Drizzle generously with 3 tbsp olive oil, making sure to coat the edges.
  3. 3
    Roast Until Crackling
    Scatter garlic and rosemary around potatoes. Season with 1 tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper. Roast at 450°F for 25–30 min until the edges are deep golden and crackling. Do not disturb while roasting.
Arugula Salad — Serves 2
  • 4 ozBaby arugula
  • ¼ cupWalnuts, roughly chopped
  • 3 tbspDried cranberries
  • —Lemon-Dijon vinaigrette (from pg. 1)
  • optionalParmigiano-Reggiano, shaved
  1. 1
    Toast the Walnuts
    In a dry skillet over medium heat, toast ¼ cup walnuts for 3–4 min, tossing frequently, until fragrant and lightly golden. Tip onto a board and roughly chop when cool enough to handle.
  2. 2
    Dress & Toss
    Just before plating, add arugula to a large bowl. Drizzle with enough vinaigrette to lightly coat — start with half, add more to taste. Add 3 tbsp cranberries and the toasted walnuts. Toss once gently.
To Plate
Build the plate in layers. Arrange a generous mound of dressed arugula as the base of a wide, shallow bowl or plate. Fan the sliced steak over the top — overlap the slices slightly so they cascade across the greens. Scatter any remaining walnuts and cranberries. Spoon a little extra vinaigrette directly over the beef. The residual heat from the steak will gently wilt the arugula at the edges — this is intentional and beautiful. Serve the smashed potatoes alongside, still crackling. Move fast; arugula waits for no one.
Make Ahead
Potatoes can be boiled and smashed up to 2 hours ahead — hold at room temperature on the sheet pan and roast when you're ready to cook the steak. The vinaigrette keeps in a sealed jar for 3 days; shake well before using.
Wine at the Table
A bright, medium-bodied red with good acidity is the move — Barbera d'Asti, Côtes du Rhône, or a lighter Grenache. The lemon vinaigrette needs fruit-forward, not grip. Avoid heavy tannic Cabernets, which will clash with the arugula's bitterness.
